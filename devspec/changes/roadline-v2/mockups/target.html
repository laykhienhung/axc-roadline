What this must achieve

  1. 01Standardise who gets an AI account, why, and how much they may spend.
  2. 02Make sure a person always reviews AI-produced work before it is used, and that someone is named as responsible.
  3. 03Set clear rules on what company data may and may not be put into AI tools.
  4. 04Agree with HR how AI skill is reflected in performance reviews and in hiring.
  5. 05Score every project on a fixed schedule, not only when asked.
  6. 06Turn each round of scoring into an action plan with a named owner - a score with no action is wasted.
  7. 07Report the company-wide picture on one dashboard that management can read at a glance.

Depends on

  • T1The shared tooling applies the data rule technically
  • T2The review gate is written into the development checklist, and the champions run the assessments
  • HRJoint owner of the performance-review and hiring criteria

Risks & mitigation

The review gate is skipped under deadline pressure Compliance audited in Q3; the control score explicitly measures whether the gate holds under pressure
Self-assessment inflation Verification and cross-project calibration before any score is published
Cost grows faster than the value returned Cost per active user is tracked against budget, not absolute spend

How we will do it

  • Account registry recording owner, role, purpose, project, quota and last-active date.
  • Usage and cost reporting automated from the provider console.
  • Request, approve, provision and review as one documented process, with a quarterly reclaim cycle.
  • A review gate saying which output types must be checked before use, who checks, and where it is recorded.
  • An accountability matrix mapping every AI output type to one named owner.
  • Self-assessment combined with verification, feeding a one-page dashboard.
  • Account and usage data feeds the assessment directly, so projects declare less by hand.
